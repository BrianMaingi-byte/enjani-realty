Off-Plan vs Ready Units: What to Weigh Before You Commit

Off-plan units get sold on the same pitch across Nairobi: pay now, at a lower price, and let the value grow while the building goes up around your investment. That pitch is true often enough to keep the model popular — but it only holds if the unit that gets delivered matches what you paid for, on a timeline you can actually plan around.

Where the savings actually come from

A developer selling off-plan is financing part of construction with buyer deposits instead of a bank loan. That's real savings they can pass on, which is why off-plan pricing usually sits meaningfully below comparable ready units nearby. The savings are genuine — the question is what you're trading for them.

What to check before you pay a deposit

Three things matter more than the brochure: the developer's track record on past projects, the approvals actually in place for this one, and the payment schedule's relationship to construction milestones.

A developer who has delivered two or three projects on or near schedule is a very different bet from a first-time developer with a striking rendering and no completed buildings. Ask for the addresses of their past projects and go look at them — not the marketing photos, the actual buildings, ideally with a current resident willing to talk.

Confirm the National Construction Authority registration and that the land has a clean title free of disputes. Your advocate should be doing this as a matter of course, not as an upsell.

Finally, look at how payments are staged. A schedule tied to verified construction milestones protects you far better than one where the bulk of the money is due upfront regardless of progress on site.

Ready units trade savings for certainty

A ready unit costs more per square metre, but you're buying something you can inspect, measure and move into — or rent out — immediately. For buyers who need income from day one, or who simply can't absorb a delayed handover, that certainty is often worth the premium.

Our rule of thumb

We tell clients to treat off-plan pricing as compensation for real risk, not free value. If the discount versus a comparable ready unit doesn't feel large enough to cover a realistic delay of six to twelve months, the off-plan unit usually isn't worth it. If it does — and the developer's track record backs it up — it can be one of the better entry points into a suburb you couldn't otherwise afford.
